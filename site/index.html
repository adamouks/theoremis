§1What it does

From first conjecture to the final

Theoremis holds the whole arc of mathematical work in one place: the exploration, the argument, and the scrutiny it has to survive.

(i)
Explore conjectures
State a conjecture, test it numerically, and study the structures behind it before committing to a proof.
Explore
(ii)
Develop proofs
Write in a typeset document with numbered results, aligned equations and cross-references that stay correct as the argument grows.
TeX
(iii)
Question assumptions
Ask the collaborator to challenge a step, search for counterexamples, or argue the other side.
Challenge
(iv)
Refine reasoning
See where an argument is thin, and compare your original step with a proposed revision before deciding.
Find gaps
(v)
Collaborate in place
Select a line, ask, review and continue. The conversation stays attached to the mathematics it concerns.
Ask⌘L
(vi)
Check in Lean 4
Formalize a step and check it against Mathlib on your computer. A proof Lean accepts can mark the step verified.

§5Formal checking

Check it in Lean 4. BETA

Formalize a step and Theoremis checks it with Lean 4 against Mathlib, on your computer. If Lean accepts the proof and nothing in it rests on sorry, you can mark the step verified. If not, Lean's errors go back to the collaborator in one click.

01FormalizeThe collaborator writes the step as a Lean 4 theorem that imports Mathlib.
02Check with LeanLean runs on your computer and reports errors, any sorry, and the axioms each proof uses.
03Mark it verifiedA proof Lean accepts can move the step to VERIFIED, checked in Lean.

Dashed, because it is new: setup installs Lean and downloads Mathlib (about 5 GB), and a first formalization often needs a few rounds of fixes.

§6Principles

Mathematicians should not be replaced by AI. They should be empowered by better tools.

Axiom 1 (Open). Theoremis is free to use, and its source is public. Read it, audit it, extend it.
Axiom 2 (Local). Your documents live on your machine, in files you own.
Axiom 3 (For mathematicians). Typeset documents, numbered results and a review process designed around proofs.
Axiom 4 (Your model). Bring your own AI: your ChatGPT plan, or open models running on your machine with Ollama.
Axiom 5 (Agency). The collaborator proposes; the mathematician decides. Nothing enters a proof without consent.
Axiom 6 (Rigor). Model reasoning stays marked unverified until something stronger checks it.

§7Status

Theoremis is in beta.

Everything here works and is ready to try on real mathematics, but it is early. So that you know where the edges are:

(i)
It will change
Features, screens and the way documents are saved may change between versions. Updates install themselves.
Updates
(ii)
Expect rough edges
Some things will break. Keep your own copies of work that matters; your documents are plain files on your computer.
Back up
(iii)
The collaborator is not a proof checker
Replies from ChatGPT or Ollama are model reasoning, marked unverified. Only Lean checks anything.
NOT VERIFIED
(iv)
Lean 4 is the newest part
Setup downloads several gigabytes and needs Git, and the first check after a restart can take a minute.
